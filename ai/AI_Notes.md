I used AI throughout the project for planning, research, checking data, portfolio modelling, sentiment analysis, creating figures, developing the Streamlit app, testing and drafting parts of the report. It was especially useful for quickly trying different methods such as HRP, Black–Litterman, Minimum CVaR and Ledoit–Wolf covariance estimation. I also used it while developing the sentiment model and building the app.

The outputs still needed a lot of checking. Some early suggestions introduced timing and look-ahead issues, incorrect assumptions, unclear chart labels and confusing metrics. The first versions of the app also misunderstood some fairly basic product requirements. For example, one design treated a fixed fee as something the user could control and gave technical details too much space. I corrected these problems through testing, visual checks and more specific instructions.

AI made the development process much faster and allowed me to try more ideas than I would have been able to test manually in the same amount of time. At the same time, I found that it was less reliable for layout decisions, product interpretation and designing the app from an investor’s point of view. I therefore treated its outputs as starting points rather than final answers and checked the important modelling, design and reporting decisions myself.
